Additionally, I looked into allocation strategies - so instead of picking which coins, look more into how much I would invest. So, when recent volatility is high, hold less, and when it would calm down, hold more. I had two controls in this - one being a constant exposure version, and then an inverted version that does the opposite, lever up into volatility. This actually beat the matched benchmark on Confirmation, and it looked at first like a win - however the second control, the inverted control, also passed. This completely disqualified the strategy, because if the volatility targeting actually worked, it's inverse shouldn't. The inverted strategy actually showed the highest raw return out of any of the tests, but it failed the placebo test by the largest amount, and it goes to show that asking for raw returns as a measure of success doesn't work with this model.
