The model runs on a schedule, where github hosts the model, and then cronjob makes sure that it runs every hour. The model covers Bitcoin, Solana, and Etherium - on which it can only go long, it doesn't short, as it was shown that it loses money. The price bars are 4 Hours and they come from Binance, specifically Binance.US To produce a score, it first goes through a "squeeze" where it computes Bollinger bands and Keltner Channels on the aformentioned 4 hour bars. When the Bollinger bands sit inside Keltner Channels, the market is unusually compressed. Here it measures how tight ( bandwidth over as a percentile over a 120 bar lookback), how long that it has been compressed, and how it broke. Then it moves onto the Adanos API, which measures market sentiment from all sorts of social media such as reddit and X, this can only dampen or lower the score and never raises it. This data is worked in as a multiplier between .5 and 1. The API gate failed, and kept multiplying the score by 1, and therefore wasn't dampening numbers. The third and final step, is the index and indicator gate. This was removed on September 1st 2026, due to it being ruled as ineffective. Essentially what it did though, was to take a couple of indicators such as VIX and the dollar index, and data from Yahoo, and apply it to the gated score from steps 1 and 2. It was worked in as .6 x step1-2 score + .4x step3 score. The two tiers of this model are Buy and Strong buy. A buy requires a score of 60 or higher, and then a strong buy being 75 and higher. In order for a signal to become a trade, it has to pass the "confirmation rule," which essentially says that having a buy on one day isn't enough to pass for a trade, it has to show up on two consecutive days - this second day is when entry occurs. Once a trade opens, the exit rules are as follows: for stop it's 1.5x ATR and then target is 3x ATR, however a strong buy can be 1.33x that 3x on a "buy." On 9-01-26, I removed the third step of the model, which was the index step, this was removed on ablation grounds. It still remains in the shadow arm so I can compare both versions.
It's the frozen CSV's of 4 Hour bars on BTC ETH and SOL as well as daily frames for the 82-ticker basket so every backtest can run on the same inputs regardless of when it executes. The frozen 4 Hour Bars was exported on 8-27-26 day and runs to 9-23-2019 on BTC and ETH with 15,117 bars per ticker, while SOL starts later at 9-18-20. The one hour bar covers around 60 thousand bars across all tickers. Each frozen dataset carries a manifest that records the freeze date, tickers, bar counts, spans, and the producing commit. Some manifests record file hashes taken at freeze time; the three core price manifests were hashed later, on September 2nd, and the manifests note this so the difference can be seen. The lockbox splits the time that any dataset looks at into two, and the first 6 months are sealed, and it is never read by the research code. The data is split into two, the first half is where the code looks for a discovery or something that works, and then it tests it on the sealed 6 months, and this is the confirmation needed for a discovery to be worked into the actual model.
The daily collector job collects data that essentially disappears after a set period of time, with the Kraken models taking data that lasts 365 days, and OKX tracking the last 180 days. Kraken funding tracks perpetual funding rates, hourly, over a 365-day window, and Kraken tickers track open interest, mark price, and finally index price for all instruments. OKX funding performs similarly to Kraken funding but over a 90 day window, and OKX open interest performs similarly to Kraken tickers, but over a 180 day window. Then there is Deribit Options, which tracks implied volatility and put/call open interest, as well as a 16 day realized volatility series. Finally, the binance_funding is a one time static backfill from the Binance archive with 6.6 years of funding history - not on daily job since its recorded all the data. It is not included in the collector job due to it already complete. Missing a single day matters because it's data that you can't remeasure, and it's a day lost that you can't test against in the future.
