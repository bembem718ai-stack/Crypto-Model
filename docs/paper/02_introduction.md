The thing that made finding a fake edge in market data so easy is the fact that essentially anyone can run a backtest. By testing enough rules, one will be profitable or win by just chance alone. With 20 tests at a normal 95% confidence level, one could appear to pass based on just pure chance alone. So, most retail trading research doesn't correct this whatsoever, so the pool of strategies is just a bunch of strategies that can't be detected as being only noise.
Academic finance has already established a lot about this, for one, there were two people Bailey and López de Prado who developed the idea of a deflated Sharpe ratio to account for how many trials actually produced a result. A few others such as Harvey, Liu, and Zhu argued that a lot of the published factors weren't real and the field should have a much higher bar. Then, McLean and Pontiff measured published anomalies, these lost roughly half of their returns after their publications {verify citation}.
