Before running an experiment, you write down what is the "bar" - so this would be things like the hypothesis number, the exact rule every constatn, the pass condition, and now the payer. This is all written in "docs/cleanroom.md".
It is important to do this because people naturally would bend the bar to make something that was a failure, turn into a success, and when there is no "bar" written in the aformentioned file, then failure could possible be bent into successes.
Failed results are recorded too.
The rule is that you cannot change the hypothesis after results exist, as this would compromise the point of finding a solution, a bent bar isn't the same bar. By changing the hypothesis, we bend the bar, and thus the results would be seen as bent, and not provide the same value as a result from an unchanged, and uncompromised hypothesis - this is why docs/cleanroom.md is such an important part of the model.
The placebo is a test to make sure that there actually is a signal, because when I was testing my model, the period I was testing on was one of particular bullishness, and thus, it's highly likely for a model to make money, so the placebo places random signals to make sure that my model is actually producing a signal - rather than just being present in a buy centered crypto market.
The question of quantifying the model simply just by "did it make money?" doesn't really apply, as the market that we tested on, and the market that I would be applying the model to if deployed, is one where crpyto is in a largely bullish position. So, just by merely being a bystander, you would make money - and if we're trying to find a signal, something where you could just be a bystander and be a "success," makes the question redundant.
Episode matching is essentially where the model groups multiple trade signals generated by the squeeze into a series of episodes, in order to make the distinction between single observations and actual events. Thus, the placebo draws random runs matching the observed run length distribution, so it carries the same clustering as the strategy and only the timing is different.
A verdict is decided against the placebo by running thousands of placebos across different times to form a distribution of outcomes under random timing. The model has to be able to be better than the higher percentiles of the placebos to be able to considered an actual signal, and if it underperforms the higher percentiles, then it's no better than just being and bystander in the market. It has to beat the placebos on two windows, only one is a failure - it has to beat on Discovery and Observation ( tested data, then lockbox data).
The lockbox rule is a vital part of the model, which is where the data that the squeeze, or any part of testing in the model, is split into two. One half, where it just observes, and then 6 months seperate from the observatory data, is left sealed, and only tested later. Each of these halves serve as a window that the signal data has to outperform the placebo on. One win on observation data, but a loss on lockbox data is an overall failure.
A fold is a portion of one of the halves, and when testing on each of the halves, the data is split into these things, where you can see the models actual edge more accurately. If one of the folds has a really high edge, but the rest of the folds don't, the average of the halve will be high, but since it's heavily dependent on one fold the edge really isn't there. A fold only counts if it produces more than 10 trades, because any less is just noise - "ex_best" calculates the average across the folds, and it wont calculate if there are less than 30 trades.
Unmeasurable is when a strategy produces less than 30 trades across the halve, since each fold needs to produce at least 10 trades. This signal would be unmeasurable by construction since "ex_best" wouldn't exist. It isn't necessarily failed, it just didn't produce enough trades to actually measure it.
A placebo has to be the exact same to the strategy of the model, except for the thing that you are testing for, because then the placebo and model are testing for something you aren't looking for.
