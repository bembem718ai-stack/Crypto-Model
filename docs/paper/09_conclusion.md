Overall, the project has found a number of quantatative results; 256 registered programs, seven axes, with and zero supported edge claims. No arrangement of public price and volume data could produce a signal that beat the random timing on a placebo under the same rules. The contribution, or actual outcome of this model was the audit methodology and the six documented failure modes, not the actual success or edge of the strategy.
The finding most likely to generalize is the fact that the null is the experiment. Most people would build a strategy and check if it makes money. The harder work is actually building the thing to compare against, and that four of the defects and major problems within the model, were in the null, not in the strategy and model. This is the hard part, the building of a model is just the shell of what goes into it - and it may be hard to come up with a concept for a model, but really what it comes down to is your own discipline, and ability to find a way to test against your model.
